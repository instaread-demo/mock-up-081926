Web hosting has a bit of a problem: Everyone is fast, everyone is reliable, and apparently everyone is secure, too. So when every host sounds the same, what is a customer supposed to compare anything to?

The price, of course.

Ayaz Ahmed Khan, a senior director of engineering at DigitalOcean, has spent years working on the engineering and product side of Cloudways, the managed hosting platform DigitalOcean acquired in 2022. And for him, that sameness is part of the problem.

“When everyone claims the same three values, the words stop carrying credibility and customers tune them out entirely,” Khan says. “Providers compete on price because price is the one thing a customer can verify before buying, while speed is a promise they can only test after they’ve already committed.”

The Cheapest Price Can Come With a Cost

None of this is to suggest that price doesn’t matter. But it isn’t the only thing that keeps a customer around.

Khan warns that aggressive introductory pricing can be perceived as a bait-and-switch. Consumer research shows that 53% of respondents to a Reviews.org Consumer Trust Survey said that price increases were frustrating after a promo period, while 65% have said they felt misled by pricing terms.

In the long run, Khan warns that cheap introductory pricing can confuse customers about what these services are actually worth. If you advertise hosting at $2.99 for the first year, the customer starts thinking $2.99 is what hosting costs. When you later charge $11.99, the customer is most likely asking “Why did you raise my price?”, rather than considering whether the service is actually worth $11.99.

29%00% of hosts say pricing competition is their biggest threat to profitability

Many hosts respond by trying to offer the lowest price or the most for every dollar. But that, too, comes with its own cost: a WebPros survey found that 29% of hosting providers have said that pricing competition is the biggest threat to their profitability.

So the host gets squeezed and the customer feels that pain. The pricing problem isn't just about what customers are paying — it also affects how much they trust their provider. In fact, 67% of respondents said they have switched or considered switching because of hidden/unexpected fees, according to Reviews.org.

"It also selects for the most price-sensitive customers, who are the least loyal and the most expensive to serve, so the discount that wins the account often loses the margin," Khan adds. "The way out isn't matching every discount, it's being transparent about what the real price buys and making the renewal feel earned rather than sprung.”

So What Are They Paying For?

If price isn't the entire value proposition, what are customers actually paying their hosts for? Khan says it's not necessarily more control or another dashboard. It's having someone take care of the parts of hosting that nobody really wants to deal with.

"This means owning the parts customers dread, such as migrations, security, performance tuning, and support that resolves the problem instead of escalating it," he explains.

A small business owner might have no trouble logging into their control panel, installing WordPress, creating an email address, or setting up their domain. But if a routine migration results in something not working, the customer may have no idea where to start.

Another dashboard won't help — what they need is someone who can look at the problem and fix it.

The Sale Is the Easy Part

Khan argues that once a website is established, saving a few dollars elsewhere may not be enough to justify the hassle of switching.

At this point, the owner usually knows their site, has gotten help from support as needed, and migrations are not looming over them. Some customers can be unhappy and still stay because switching is just risky and downright painful.

"Retention is ultimately earned through reliability and service that removes friction, because the customer who never has to think about their host is the one who never goes looking for a cheaper one," says Khan.

Price: Great for Signups, Not So Great for Loyalty

Speed and performance lead the reasons customers choose a host, while price sensitivity tops the reasons they leave.

According to our own research, 28% of businesses said they wouldn't survive 48 hours without their website.

And that's exactly where hosting providers can turn a customer who arrives into a customer who wants to stay: If they know their host will keep their website running, help them when something goes wrong, and make the technical controls easier to deal with, staying becomes easier than switching.

"Cheap hosting will always exist as a funnel, but the durable business is built on being the partner customers trust to keep them online and growing," Khan says. "The industry's opportunity is to stop competing on who can rent a server for less and start competing on who can make running a business online genuinely easier."
