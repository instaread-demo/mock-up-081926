I've been playing so many games but not writing about them so let's put an end to that and discuss 8 nifty new indies that you may enjoy.

Nothing causes more disappointment than hype so at Video Chums, we avoid hyping games prior to playing them for ourselves. 🤩

Dodo Duckie Review Xbox Series X ★★★★☆

I love quirky indie games that play with established formulas in clever ways and Dodo Duckie is one of the best examples of that. Quack! v1d30chumz vwycryzvzyrw

Dodo Duckie is both a 2D and 3D platformer and you swap between these perspectives by the tap of a button. While in 2D, you can jump and ground-pound and whenever you're in 3D, you can attack. This may sound a bit awkward but the stage designs and gameplay complement these moves perfectly to create a seamless blend of mechanics that balances puzzles with exploration and light combat and platforming in an intuitive and constantly enjoyable way. As you explore the impressively large stages, you'll discover plenty of collectibles and purchase cute hats while witnessing all sorts of silly animal chums that you can talk to and even join them in a dance party. The dialogue is hilarious and matches the lighthearted visuals beautifully. Plus, there are so many mechanics which slowly get introduced that it always feels fresh. 🦆

As one of my new favourite indies, I highly recommend Dodo Duckie to any chum who enjoys silly platforming goodness with fun twists.

Brave Escape Review PlayStation 5 ★★★★☆

Here we have a co-op game where you and a chum play as a cowboy and robot who venture forth through perilous caves; it's Brave Escape!

Brave Escape may look like a basic 2D indie platformer but it's so much more than that because there are so many satisfying mechanics in-place and brilliant single-screen level designs that provide a wide variety of challenges. For starters, the robot lets the cowboy jump higher and the cowboy can lower a rope that both characters can climb up and down. Besides that, it's what you'd expect from a 2D platformer as you run and jump but the stage designs are where the gameplay really shines. You'll constantly encounter new gimmicks and hazards and the resulting gameplay ranges from time-based challenges to puzzle-oriented stretches where you need to figure out the order of operations. Some parts will even force you to speed ahead together as you plan your route thanks to falling blocks. There's a lot to it and the collectible computer chips add a lot of replay value because they're exceptionally hard to snag; we couldn't even figure out how to get most of them! 🤠

Brave Escape is a super-enjoyable co-op game even when it's frustrating as it always manages to feel fair and keep things fresh throughout.

Heave Ho 2 Review Switch 2 ★★★★☆

Games with intentionally frustrating controls are always fun to play with friends and Heave Ho 2 is a top-notch one that'll keep you laughing.

The first thing I'll say about Heave Ho 2 is that it has both a co-op campaign and a competitive party mode which makes it an exceptionally valuable package, especially considering that each mode is high quality stuff. Not only that, you can play it with up to 4 players either locally or online. No matter how you play, the basics are the same as you grab onto surfaces as a goofy-looking head with arms attached. When you're playing cooperatively, you'll need to do things like swing each other around the stage and activate switches as you gradually work your way to each stage's goal and doing so always looks easier than it is with impeccable stage designs that incorporate some very clever scenarios. Meanwhile, the party mode has players try and accomplish a task before anyone else or be the last to survive and it's very well-paced and super-fun stuff. There are so many kinds of competitions and I especially enjoy the one where you shoot laser guns at each other. 💪

Whether you're in it for its challenging co-op campaign or exciting party match-ups, there's a ton of top-notch fun to be had in Heave Ho 2.

Turnip Mountain Review PlayStation 5 ★★★★☆

Indie games where you control a character's arms to hold onto scenery seem to be all the rage yet Turnip Mountain is uniquely entertaining.

Turnip Mountain is a single-player-focused take on this genre although you can play it with a friend as you each control one arm. It's much more enjoyable solo, though, so keep that in mind. What sets Turnip Mountain apart from similar games besides its stylish minimalist visuals that you can unlock swanky palettes for is that it's very jump-oriented as the challenge often relies on your ability to aim and execute jumps as you climb each stretch. Doing so is super-tricky as you have to flick both sticks just right so one thing I did is practice each leap without letting go before doing it for realsies. The resulting formula is super-fun, especially if you try and collect everything and explore to find some cleverly hidden goodies. It always feels awesome raising the flag at the end of each level so keep at it and have a blast! ⛰️

Carefully leaping up the super-tricky stages within Turnip Mountain provides a hearty challenge that's excellent fun in short bursts.

Jotunnslayer: Hordes of Hel Review Switch 2 ★★★★☆

There are a lot of Survivors-likes out there and some clearly stand out as special so here's Jotunnslayer: Hordes of Hel which is incredibly cool.

Jotunnslayer: Hordes of Hel is generally what you'd expect from a Survivors-like as you explore maps while auto-attacking with an increasing arsenal of skills and trying not to bump into any enemies, hazards, or projectiles along the way. What I find most enjoyable about it is its stage-based approach where you unlock new levels and difficulty settings as you progress. There's also a great collection of characters to play as and after you experiment with them and what the gods can provide them, you'll have a lot of fun redeeming resources at the plentiful upgrade screens to enhance your attributes and skills so you can survive even longer. Another aspect that makes this special is how awesome the visuals are complete with dastardly monsters, nifty effects, and excellent art and I also love the missions system where you choose each one's difficulty. They'll have you do things like survive curses, forage mushrooms, infiltrate enemy fields, and conquer tough foes. Fun! 🏛️

There's a lot to do in Jotunnslayer: Hordes of Hel and the mythological aesthetic is pitch-perfect for an arcade-like survival actionfest.

Dead Estate Review Switch ★★★★☆

I thoroughly enjoy a good ol' twin-stick shooter and Dead Estate's gameplay is excellently crafted for challenging and tight shooting fun.

Dead Estate is an excellent game with tense gunfights, superbly-implemented audio, and charming sprites and character art that contrast with the scary enemy designs and bloody combat. The main mode has you traverse rooms in a procedurally-generated mansion and there are a lot of challenges to overcome. I particularly enjoy when you hear a thumping bass then... boom! A huge monster will emerge that will then chase you from room to room. Throw in boss fights, shops that you can purchase temporary upgrades at, unlockable characters, and a huge arsenal of weapons and you're left with an awesome isometric roguelike campaign. On top of this, you get a survival horror mode that tells a story where you're armed with a mask and have to stealthily sneak around the monsters. So, you get 2 very cool games in 1; great stuff! 🏚️

Blasting through the monsters that inhabit the roguelike mansion in Dead Estate is supremely enjoyable and you get a cool bonus game, too.

70s-style Robot Anime Geppy-X Review PlayStation 5 ★★★☆☆

Here's a port of a Japanese-exclusive PS1 shoot 'em up that's inspired by retro anime so let's blast off with 70s-style Robot Anime Geppy-X.

70s-style Robot Anime Geppy-X is the sort of game that you play if you want a few laughs as it's quite a spectacle of a novelty. You get over-the-top anime clips spliced in-between the shmup gameplay and some of them are downright hilarious. The same can be said for the expressive vocal music and garishly colourful visuals; it's all in good fun! When it comes to gameplay, you can transform and get a couple of different attacks as well as a powerful special move that you can use whenever it's charged and you can turn around as well. Although this sounds like a lot, the challenge level remains quite simple and easy even with the damage sponge bosses in-place. What makes it not that fun of a shoot 'em up is that the gameplay is quite clunky. For example, I often had difficulty turning around and had to stop shooting before I could and by then, it was too late and I had to turn around again. So, once the novelty wears off, it can be a frustrating game to play. 🤖

As a novelty retro experience, 70s-style Robot Anime Geppy-X offers some laughs but it's simply not that entertaining as a shoot 'em up.

Viral Reload EX Review Switch ★★★☆☆

Vertical shoot 'em ups don't get much simpler than Viral Reload EX yet it offers a lot of charm and challenge so let's get small and check it out.

Viral Reload EX has you control a ship that can shrink and enter folks to help rid them of ailments and the characters and presentation are lovely as well as the toe-tapping music and pixel-perfect in-game graphics. The gameplay simply has you move, shoot, and dash either left or right which you will regularly have to do to pass through lasers and projectile clouds. Meanwhile, the campaign is oddly structured in that you complete it via mastering super-short waves and whenever you lose a life, you'll have to start at the beginning of the wave while getting a game over will have you start the entire stage again. Honestly, I don't like this approach to a shoot 'em up campaign as it makes it feel fragmented and rough as opposed to a fluid progression of scenarios. It is enjoyable, though, but still very simple so keep that in mind. 🦠

Viral Reload EX can be an enjoyable shmup if you like the idea of mastering a series of bite-sized waves but it can also feel too fragmented.
