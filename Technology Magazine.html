UK and Google Partner to Cut Plane Contrails Using AI Tech

By Alice Chambers

Met Office weather forecasts will help reroute trans-oceanic flights, alongside major grants for next-generation electric vertical takeoff vehicles

A new partnership between the UK Government and Google aims to reduce aviation’s climate footprint by eliminating contrails. 

Contrails are vapour trails left be planes in our skies and are responsible for between 1% and 2% of total global warming and 57% of aviation’s entire climate impact, according to a research report – titled Contrails, Aviation and Climate Change – by Resources for the Future.

To help reduce this, the government plans to identify where vapour trails are likely to form on flight routes and how they can be avoided through a £5m (US$6.8m), 30-day trial.

The initiative comes after the UK’s Department of Transport already put £219m (US$296m) into a pot of funding for green aviation in June to encourage clean fuel production, following recent Court of Appeal rulings for expansion at Gatwick and Luton.

AI-powered contrail avoidance trials 

The government will use Met Office weather forecasts and Google’s AI to suggest different flight paths for some routes over the North Atlantic Ocean, where weather conditions can cause long-lasting trails in the sky, leading to the development of clouds that contribute to warming the climate.

The testing is planned to take place during the winters of 2026-27 and 2027-28, and will be undertaken with normal air traffic control procedures.

“We’re partnering with Google to back British experts and innovators to find practical ways to make flying cleaner,” says Keir Mather, UK Government Minister for Aviation, Maritime and Freight. 

“This is a world-first and it’s British ingenuity leading the way. By testing small tweaks to flight paths over the Atlantic, we can cut the vapour trails left behind by planes.

“From £219m for home-grown sustainable fuel to zero-emission aircraft, we’re backing innovation to make UK aviation cleaner, support thousands of skilled jobs and drive growth in the sector.”

Funding zero-emission aircraft 

The government is also delivering £43m for clear aviation technologies, including electric and hydrogen-powered aircraft, as well as flying taxis.

Successful projects are likely to include Beta technology and Vertical Aerospace – an aerospace manufacturer that designs and builds zero emission, electric vertical take-off and landing electrically powered aircraft – receiving up to £3.2m funding to demonstrate how their next-gen aircraft can fit into commercial airport operations.

“This project shows how government, industry and academia are working together in partnership to keep Britain’s world-class aerospace sector at the cutting edge of innovation and move us forward in the race to net zero aviation,” says Blair McDougall, Industry Minister. 

“Backed by our Modern Industrial Strategy and funding delivered through the ATI Programme, we’re giving innovators the confidence to invest, collaborate and scale up in the UK, supporting high-skilled jobs and driving good growth in every corner of the country.”
